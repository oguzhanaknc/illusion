# 0048. Wind: a scene Component, vertex displacement per Material, particles that follow it

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-07

## Context

Outdoor scenes look dead when nothing moves: trees, grass and smoke should react to wind. The renderer
has no vertex animation, and meshes carry no per-vertex weights (the vertex is position, normal, tangent and
UV). Wind must work in the editor (Scene view) and in games, keep TAA and shadows stable, and not need
special meshes.

Options considered:

- **Skinned or baked animation.** Accurate, but it needs authored bones or textures per asset and does not
  react to gusts.
- **Per-vertex weights in a new vertex attribute or vertex colour.** The best artistic control, but it
  changes the vertex layout and every importer.
- **Analytic displacement from the height above the object's origin, driven by Material parameters.**
  Works with any mesh whose origin is at its base (the usual case for plants), costs a few instructions per
  vertex, and Materials split a model into parts that bend (`windSway`), flutter (`windFlutter`) or stay
  rigid.

## Decision

- `Wind` (`illusion.Wind`, core, singleton) holds direction, speed, gust strength and frequency and
  turbulence. Its clock is the runtime `WindState`, advanced by `advanceWind()`; `GameSession` and the
  editor's preview call it every frame. `windGust()`/`windVelocity()` in C++ and the same functions in
  `Common.slang` define one model: gust fronts that travel downwind at the wind's speed.
- `MaterialData` gains `windSway` and `windFlutter` (cooked model format version 2). The forward vertex
  shader, which the depth prepass also runs, displaces vertices by the height above the instance origin
  squared (sway) plus a per-vertex jitter (flutter), for this frame's time and, for motion vectors, the
  previous frame's. The shadow caster does the same; casters with wind are batched by Material.
- `ParticleEmitter::windFactor` blends particle velocities toward the wind velocity at the emitter, on the
  CPU and, through the published GPU step, in the compute shader.

## Consequences

- No new vertex attributes or importers; plants need their origin at their base and separate Materials for
  parts that move differently.
- Culling bounds do not grow with the displacement; tall swaying objects can be culled a few centimetres
  early at the screen edge.
- Normals are not bent; the shading of strongly bent geometry is slightly off.
- One wind per scene; local wind zones would need a new decision.
